Emotional voice in ASL translation

Studying expressive synthesized speech in Deaf-to-hearing communication.

February–September 2024 · Research Assistant
Cornell University · SciFi Lab
With Professor Cheng Zhang and PhD student Hyunchul Lim

Overview

Sign-to-speech translation should communicate how someone expresses themselves, alongside what they say. This project investigated whether synthesized emotional speech can help hearing non-signers understand an ASL signer’s emotional intent.

Research question

ASL facial expressions convey both emotion and linguistic information. A facial expression marking intensity, for example, can be mistaken for anger. Can emotional voice help listeners distinguish these signals?

Study approach

We compared ASL videos accompanied by subtitles, neutral synthesized speech, or emotionally expressive synthesized speech. The stimuli varied emotional expressions, linguistic markers, and their order.

Hearing participants identified emotions and sign meanings and rated perceived effort. Feedback from Deaf and hard-of-hearing participants informed the design of emotional voice features.

My contribution

  • Developed the emotional neural text-to-speech pipeline and prepared speech stimuli.
  • Implemented randomized Qualtrics studies and aligned synthesized speech with ASL videos.
  • Processed approximately 100 ASL videos for controlled experimental presentation.
  • Conducted statistical analysis and visualization, including condition comparisons and emotion-confusion matrices.

Key findings

  • Emotional speech improved emotion recognition compared with subtitles and neutral speech, including when emotional and linguistic expressions appeared together.
  • Benefits varied by emotion; happiness remained difficult to recognize.
  • Clearer emotional cues did not produce a statistically significant improvement in understanding sign meanings in the reported comparison.

Design implications

Accessible translation should preserve expressive intent while distinguishing emotional cues from linguistic markers. Emotional voice can support interpretation, but its clarity and alignment with signed expression matter.
